# Figure captions

**Figure 1 | Score-specific observed risks and differences by diagnosis position.** Rows: eICU-CRD and MIMIC-IV; columns: sepsis and pneumonia. Panels show observed in-hospital mortality per admission record (top), δ_s = risk_never,s − risk_primary,s in percentage points (pp; middle), and stacked record counts (bottom). Whiskers show pointwise 95% patient-cluster confidence intervals (CIs). Gray bands and open δ_s circles mark CIs containing zero; diamonds mark point estimates without CIs. Risk and δ_s intervals use separate reporting criteria. At pneumonia score zero, primary deaths/records are 0/17 (eICU) and 0/13 (MIMIC-IV); these CIs are withheld. δ_s is score-specific; Table 2 reports the record-weighted summary Δ_w. Scores are operational reconstructions, not simultaneous baseline severity assessments. qSOFA, quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment; CURB-65, confusion, urea, respiratory rate, blood pressure and age ≥65 years.

**Figure 2 | Held-out Brier-loss differences between primary-only and broad-cohort risk mappings.** Panels show all test records (A), primary test records (B), and never-primary test records (C), using the source-specific diagnosis-position definitions. Each row is one source–condition application. Circles compare the original score-based mappings; squares compare mappings after independent intercept recalibration on a common broad cohort. Values are 1,000 × (Brier loss of the primary-trained mapping − Brier loss of the broad-trained mapping); negative values favor the primary-trained mapping. Whiskers are pointwise paired patient-cluster 95% percentile confidence intervals (CIs), conditional on the fitted and recalibrated mappings; model-development uncertainty is excluded. Hospital-cluster sensitivities, all contrasts, test sizes and calibration metrics are in Supplementary Tables 23–26. The eICU pneumonia hospital-cluster CI before recalibration includes zero. These exploratory comparisons measure prediction loss; δ_s and the record-weighted summary Δ_w quantify mortality-risk differences. Mapping performance is compared separately within each test population.
